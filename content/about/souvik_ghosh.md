## Bio

If you want to know more about my academic trajectory:

As a researcher, the opportunity to address fundamental unanswered questions while developing solutions with potential real-world impact has consistently motivated me and shaped my scientific interests. My interdisciplinary research journey began with the prestigious INSPIRE Fellowship, awarded by the Government of India, which enabled me to pursue an integrated BS-MS degree at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Kolkata. During my undergraduate studies, I broadened my experimental expertise through research internships in neuropsychiatric science at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc) Bangalore and in molecular biology as a member of the IISER Kolkata iGEM team. These experiences provided me with a strong foundation in experimental biology and introduced me to the interdisciplinary nature of life science research.

For my Master's research, I joined the laboratory of Prof. Dibyendu Das, an expert in the field of systems chemistry. His group seeks to recreate key features of living systems using simple chemical building blocks to understand the chemical origins of life while developing adaptive functional materials. Within this framework, I designed enzyme-powered micromotors based on self-assembled peptide nanotubes. By coupling enzymatic reaction cascades to achieve motility, directional movement, and fluorogenic responses, I demonstrated programmable, active, life-like systems capable of exhibiting dynamic, stimulus-responsive behaviour.

In September 2021, I began my Ph.D. research under the supervision of Prof. Wilhelm Huck at Radboud University in the Netherlands. Prof. Huck is an internationally recognised pioneer in systems chemistry and synthetic cell research whose work has fundamentally advanced our understanding of how complex, life-like behaviours can emerge from non-living chemical systems. During my Ph.D., I focused on designing enzymatic reaction networks, particularly for molecular information processing. I developed a new design framework for nonlinear biochemical reaction networks and demonstrated, for the first time, that enzymatic reaction networks can be used for reservoir computing and molecular sensing. Using this network architecture, I developed a glucose sensor. These achievements provided me with extensive expertise in designing and characterising complex biochemical systems.

During my Ph.D., I collaborated with researchers from different disciplines and institutions. I worked with Prof. Beves at UNSW Sydney (Australia) to integrate photoacids with enzymatic reaction networks to achieve photochemical control, and with Dr. Bin Li from the Lanzhou Institute of Chemical Physics (China) to develop a glucose sensor. I also collaborated with colleagues to develop a flow-chemistry platform coupled with mass spectrometry for analysing a glycolytic pathway reconstructed in vitro. During my Ph.D., I supervised four Master's research projects, two of which contributed to joint publications, and participated in diverse professional development activities. I also contributed to public engagement by organising two seasons of the *Late Night Conference with Wilhelm Huck*, a science-outreach series broadcast on YouTube.

In May 2026, I began my postdoctoral research with Prof. Kerstin Göpfrich at Heidelberg University, Germany. Prof. Göpfrich is an expert in RNA nanotechnology and bottom-up synthetic biology, and her group seeks to engineer synthetic cells using RNA origami. I am currently designing different types of DNA-based feedback networks to achieve temporal control over RNA expression from a de novo genome for synthetic cells. RNA structures can fold co-transcriptionally from specific genes, providing a route towards genetically encoded molecular machinery. The ultimate goal is to develop a synthetic cell that operates using molecular machinery made predominantly from RNA rather than proteins, reminiscent of a laboratory-engineered version of an "RNA world."

My broader ambition is to exploit biochemical reaction motifs for applications spanning biosynthesis, active matter, and synthetic biology. This interest is inspired by living systems, whose survival and adaptability depend on numerous biochemical reaction networks operating in a coordinated manner. I am fascinated by the complexity of these networks and by their remarkable ability to process diverse physicochemical information, make decisions, and regulate dynamic behaviour.
