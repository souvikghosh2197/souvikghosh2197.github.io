## Bio

If you want to know more about my academic trajectory:

As a researcher, the opportunity to address fundamental unanswered questions while developing solutions with potential real-world impact has consistently motivated and shaped my scientific interests. My interdisciplinary research journey began with the prestigious INSPIRE Fellowship awarded by the Government of India, which enabled me to pursue an integrated BS–MS degree at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Kolkata. During my undergraduate studies, I broadened my experimental expertises through research internships in neuropsychiatric science at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc) Bangalore and in molecular biology as a member of the IISER Kolkata iGEM team. These experiences provided a strong foundation in experimental biology and introduced me to the interdisciplinary nature of life science research. For my Master's research, I joined the laboratory of Prof. Dibyendu Das (https://www.ddaslab.com/) , an expert in the emerging field of systems chemistry. His group seeks to recreate key features of living systems using simple chemical building blocks to understand the chemical origins of life while developing adaptive functional materials. Within this framework, I designed enzyme-powered micromotors based on self-assembled peptide nanotubes. By coupling enzymatic reaction cascades to achieve motility, directional movement, and fluorogenic responses, I demonstrated programmable active, life-like systems capable of exhibiting dynamic, stimulus-responsive behaviour. 

In September 2021, I started my Ph.D. research under the supervision of Prof. Wilhelm Huck (https://www.hucklab.com/) at Radboud University, Netherlands. Prof. Wilhelm Huck is an internationally recognised pioneer in systems chemistry and synthetic cell research, whose work has fundamentally advanced our understanding of how complex life-like behaviours can emerge from non-living chemical systems. Here, I focused on designing enzymatic reaction networks, particularly for molecular information processing. I developed a new design framework for nonlinear biochemical reaction networks. This work demonstrated, for the first time, the enzymatic reaction network can be used for reservoir computing and molecular sensing. I built a glucose sensor using this network architecture. These achievements have provided me with extensive expertise in designing and characterize complex biochemical systems. Additionally, I collaborated with other groups and colleagues, including Prof. Beves at UNSW Sydney (Australia) to integrate photoacids with enzymatic reaction networks to achieve photochemical control, Dr. Bin Li from Lanzhou Institute of Chemical Physics (China) to develop glucose sensor, and collaborating with fellow colleague to develop a flowchemistry platform coupled with mass spectrometry for analysing the glycolysis pathway reconstructed in vitro. I also supervised 4 Master's research projects (resulted in joint publication), and participating in diverse professional development courses. I also contributed to public engagement during my PhD, by organising two seasons of the Late Night Conference with Wilhelm Huck, a science-outreach series broadcast on YouTube.

In May 2026, I began my postdoctoral research with Prof. Kerstin Göpfrich (https://goepfrichgroup.de/), at Heidelberg University, Germany. Prof. Kerstin Göpfrich is an expert in RNA nanotechnology and bottom-up synthetic biology, where her group attempts to engineer synthetic cells with RNA origami. I am currently designing different types of DNA-based feedback networks to achieve temporal control over RNA expression of a de-novo genome for synthetic cells. RNA structures can fold cotranscriptionally
from specific genes. The result would be a synthetic cell which operates based on a machinery made from RNA instead of proteins, reminiscent of a lab-made version of an “RNA world”.


My broader ambition is to exploit biochemical reaction motifs for applications spanning biosynthesis, active matter, and synthetic biology. This interest is inspired by living systems, whose survival and adaptability depend on numerous biochemical reaction networks operating in a coordinated manner. I am fascinated by the sheer complexity of these networks and by their ability to process diverse physicochemical information, make decisions, and regulate dynamic behaviour.
